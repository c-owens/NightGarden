# Night Garden

A small, quiet, interactive night scene that runs in your browser. Plant glowing flowers on a hillside under the stars, bring up the dawn when you're ready, brush through them with your cursor, watch the fireflies drift, and turn on a night soundscape of crickets, cicadas, a distant owl and the wind.

It's one HTML file plus a music track, with no dependencies, no install, no build step, and no internet needed. Keep the `music` folder next to the HTML files.

## How to run

- Double-click **`Open Night Garden.bat`**, or
- Double-click **`index.html`** (or drag it into any browser) to open the index of scenes, then pick one. The garden itself is `garden.html`.

Press **F11** for fullscreen. It looks best that way.

## Controls

| Input | What it does |
|---|---|
| Click / tap | Plant a flower where you clicked |
| Move the mouse | Flowers and grass bend away from the cursor and spring back; fast sweeps whip up a breeze (you'll hear it too) |
| **S** | Toggle all sound |
| **M** | Toggle music: the backing track (`music/night_garden.mp3`, looped), and each new flower plays a note in the same key |
| **N** | Toggle nature sounds: crickets, cicadas and an owl at night, birdsong in the morning, and wind |
| **D** | Dawn: the sun rises over about 45 seconds. Press again to let night fall |
| **B** | Toggle a breathing guide (4s in, 4s hold, 6s out) |
| **W** | Make a wish: a shooting star and a small note (in the morning, a bird answers instead) |
| **C** | Clear the garden |
| **H** | Hide/show the help text |

Your garden is saved in the browser, so the flowers are still there when you come back.

## The pond

Follow the **down to the pond** link (bottom-left) to `pond.html`, a moonlit pond across the water from the garden: click the water to float a lantern, click the sky to hang a star, and the koi will come to see. **S** sound, **M** music, **W** wish, **B** breathe, **C** clear your stars, **H** hide help.

The pond has its own music, `music/moon_pond.mp3`: a 4-minute piece in D major (chorused glass pads, kalimba, water drops, frogs and spring peepers), generated by `music/compose_pond.py`. Run `python compose_pond.py` (needs numpy + scipy; add `lameenc` to get the mp3 as well as the wav) to re-render it.

## The hilltop

Follow **up the hill** (bottom-left, in the garden or at the pond) to `hill.html`: lying on the grass on the hill above the garden, under a moonless sky with the Milky Way across it. You can see the garden glowing far down in the valley, and the pond beside it.

Click a star, then another, and another, to draw your own constellation. Click the last star again (or press **Enter**) to finish it, and it gets a name, like *The Unhurried Heron* or *The Kettle of the Small Hours*. Each star rings a note as you join it, higher stars ringing higher, and a finished constellation plays all of its stars in order. Hover over one to see its name; click it to hear it again.

| Input | What it does |
|---|---|
| Click a star | Start a constellation, or join the next star to it (click an earlier star to close a shape) |
| Click the last star again / **Enter** | Finish and name it |
| **Backspace** / **Esc** | Undo the last line / let the one you're drawing go |
| **R** | Rename the constellation under the cursor (or the one you just made) |
| **S** / **M** / **N** | Sound / music (a slow pad in F, generated live) / nature (wind across the hilltop, crickets far down the slope) |
| **W** | A shooting star and a small note |
| **B** | Breathing guide |
| **C** | Press twice to let all your constellations go |
| **H** | Hide/show the help text |

The sky really turns: once every three hours around the pole star, by the clock, so your constellations rise and set behind the hill and are somewhere else when you come back. They are saved in the browser, and they also appear, faintly, in the night sky over the garden and in the sky (and its reflection) at the pond, in the same place in the turning sky. (That part needs the pages to share browser storage, which they do in Chrome and Edge when opened as files.) Now and then a satellite drifts over.

## The high pond

Follow **to the high pond** (in the garden, or from the hilltop) to `high-pond.html`, where the pond and the hilltop come together: a still pond right up on the hill, under the hilltop's turning sky, with the hill's lone tree standing on the far bank and the garden glowing beyond it.

Everything from the pond is here: click the water to float a lantern, and the koi come to see; fireflies hang over the water with their reflections. And everything from the sky is here: join stars to draw constellations, exactly as on the hilltop, and the pond reflects them, along with the stars, the Milky Way and the tree. It's the same sky as the hilltop (same stars, same clock), and constellations are shared, so the ones you made on the hill are up there too, and the ones you make here are waiting on the hill.

Controls are the hilltop's for the sky (click the last star again or **Enter** to finish, **R** rename, **Backspace** undo, **Esc** cancel, **C** twice to clear) plus **S** sound, **M** music (the pond's own track, `music/moon_pond.mp3`), **W** wish, **B** breathe and **H** hide help. Star notes are tuned to the pond's key, D major.

## How it's built

- **Canvas 2D**: everything is drawn procedurally each frame. Stems are quadratic curves driven by a per-flower spring, so they bend away from the cursor, get dragged by its motion, and wobble back into place; petals are bezier shapes with gradient fills, and glows use additive blending.
- **Music**: `music/night_garden.mp3` is a 4-minute ambient piece in A minor, itself generated from code by `music/compose.py` (additive-synth pads, FM bells, synthesized crickets/cicadas/owl, convolution reverb). Run `python compose.py` (needs numpy + scipy) to re-render it.
- **Web Audio**: the live sounds are synthesized in the browser. The flower chimes are triangle waves on a pentatonic scale through a feedback delay, so any combination of notes sounds fine. Crickets are pulse-trains of a high sine wave, each with its own pitch, tempo and stereo position. Cicadas are band-passed noise buzzed by a fast oscillator and slowly swelled in and out. The owl is a few pitch-bending sine hoots sent through an echo, and the wind is filtered noise whose volume tracks how fast you move the mouse.
- **localStorage** keeps your garden between visits.
